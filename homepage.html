It's usually Sporadic-E, not solar skip

Most CB/11-meter skip is Sporadic-E (Es) — dense, patchy ionized clouds ~60 miles up that reflect 27 MHz easily. It's mostly a seasonal phenomenon (peaks May–August) and largely independent of the solar cycle. True F2-layer solar skip on 11m does happen, but only during strong solar-maximum periods with sustained high solar flux — it's the rarer of the two mechanisms on this band.

When to listen for it

Watch Channel 6 (26.965 MHz + freeband) first — nicknamed the "Superbowl channel" because that's where skip-hunters congregate. If Ch. 19 suddenly fills with unfamiliar accents and calls from three states away, that's your sign. Es tends to open in short windows, often mid-morning through evening.

Note: this outlook estimates overall favorability from public NOAA data + seasonal Es patterns. It's directional, not a minute-by-minute forecast — Sporadic-E specifically can open and close in under an hour with little warning.

What's Actually Legal

CB is one of the only two-way radio services in the U.S. you can operate with zero license, zero test, and zero fee — as long as you stay inside these limits.

4W
Max AM Power

Measured at the transmitter output. This is the ceiling for standard AM CB operation — no license needed.

12W
Max SSB (PEP)

Single-sideband is legal on channels 36–40 and gets more range per watt than AM, at the cost of needing an SSB-capable radio.

$0
License Required

Unlike GMRS ($35) or Ham (test required), CB has been license-free since 1983. Callsigns are optional, not mandatory.

Not legal: "export radios" / linear amplifiers that push CB sets past 4W/12W, and antennas taller than 60 ft (or 20 ft above the tallest structure on the property). These are the two most common ways CB operators actually draw FCC attention.

About CB Radio HQ

Hey, I'm Michael Smith. My love for radio started when I was 7 years old, and honestly, it's never stopped growing since. I'm a licensed ham radio operator, and over the years that's pulled me into pretty much every corner of the radio spectrum — CB, shortwave, GMRS, you name it. If it transmits, I'm probably interested in it.

This site is a one-man operation. No team, no investors — just someone who thought the CB hobby deserved better tools than a 2004-era forum wall of text. Everything here — the SWR Doctor, the live skip outlook, the antenna picker, the hands-on reviews — I built by hand because I kept seeing the same questions asked over and over with no straight answer.

It's not my only project. I also run hqshortwaveradio.com for the shortwave listening side of things, and pocradiohq.com for Push-to-Talk over Cellular radios — different corners of the same lifelong hobby.

Since it's just me running this, I actually read what comes in. Found a bug? Got a feature you wish existed? Think I got something wrong on the channel guide? Tell me — I'm here for any suggestions or issues. This site gets better because the people who use it tell me what's missing.

Glad you're here. Now go check your skip conditions. —Michael
